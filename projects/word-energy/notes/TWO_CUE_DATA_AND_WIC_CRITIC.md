The exact star posterior can be written `p_T(s|A,B) ∝ q_T(s) M_A(s) M_B(s)`. Therefore multiplying the two normalized one-cue posteriors and dividing by the prior, then normalizing, recovers the same two-cue posterior; calling this a separate additive posterior control would be incorrect. A window-score control can instead compare `log E_q[M_A M_B]` with `log E_q[M_A] + log E_q[M_B]` at identical spoke strengths. An uncoupled same-checkpoint ablation has identically zero normalized interaction score; retraining that as a ranking comparator is not informative. A nonlinear posterior region can decode XOR even from factorized coordinates, so accuracy alone does not establish collective dynamics.
